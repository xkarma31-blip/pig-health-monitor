## B. Test-by-test logic audit

**1. v6 I²S matrix (stereo L/R, ONLY_LEFT, ONLY_RIGHT, 16 kHz, APLL — all idle at all-ones)**
- Proves: the flatline is *not* slot selection, not legacy-driver left/right
  naming, not sample rate, not APLL clock quality. Raw 32-bit words are
  themselves all-ones, so the `>>16` conversion is also cleared as the cause.
- Confounder (driver install/uninstall churn) is neutralised: pass 2
  reproduced pass 1, and the production build (single install) shows the
  identical signature.
- **Resolved paradox #1:** independently documented ESP32-S3 behaviour —
  "−1 / 0xFFFFFFFF (line floating high) when the I²S RX bus has no data"
  (m5stack/uiflow-micropython PR #97, Cardputer-Adv ES8311 case, same chip).
  All-ones **is the documented reading of a floating data line on ESP32-S3**,
  not evidence of a short or a stuck pin.

**2. v7 GPIO6 passive + data-pin sweep**
- GPIO6 with internal pull-DOWN reads LOW (0/50). The internal pulldown
  (~45 kΩ) would lose against a short to 3V3, so **GPIO6 is not shorted and
  not actively driven — it is a floating high-impedance node.** This and the
  all-ones reading are the *same* fact seen twice.
- Sweep flaws, owned: (a) GPIO 38–42/45–48 are back-of-board castellated pads
  on the SuperMini, so constant-zeros there are meaningless; (b) activity on
  7/8/9/10 is most plausibly capacitive crosstalk from toggling WS(4)/SCK(5)
  on the same header row: values were noise-shaped (min/max full-scale,
  transition counts ≪ real audio rates); (c) **the sweep never covered front
  header GPIOs 3, 11, 12, 13** → "SD wire landed on a different pin" is
  *reduced*, not excluded.

**3. Production pipeline math (re-derived from source 2026-10-17)**
`raw(-1) >> 16 → −1 → RMS24=1, Peak24=1, nz=100% (−1 ≠ 0), −138.4 dBFS →
PCM 0xFFFF frames`. Every dashboard number mechanically derives from the
idle line; **nonzeroPct cannot distinguish "idle" from "quiet"** by design.
Dashboards showing 100% while flat are a metric bug, not new evidence.

**4. INMP441 datasheet (primary source)**
- SD tri-states outside its slot and **requires a 100 kΩ pulldown on the
  trace**; we have none → an undriven trace genuinely floats.
- **CHIPEN (pin 8): low/floating ⇒ power-down ⇒ SD tri-stated in every slot,
  at every rate, permanently.** This is the only mode reproducing our exact
  signature under all tested configurations. Whether the Master's module even
  exposes CHIPEN is unknown (6-pin breakouts tie it internally; larger ones
  expose it and must be tied to VDD).
- **ESD rule found in datasheet:** never feed WS/SCK clocks into a mic without
  VDD power — the ESD protection diodes conduct and degrade the mic. Our
  streaming firmware toggles clocks whenever it runs; any period where VDD
  was lifted (soldering) while the ESP ran clocks = electrical stress. This is
  a plausible damage mechanism for this session and is now a standing rule.
- Startup: mic outputs zero for 2¹⁸ SCK cycles (~85 ms) after VDD — not our
  issue at 48k/44k continuous runs.

**5. SuperMini silkscreen ground**
Front-header labels IO1–IO13 map 1:1 to GPIO numbers (espboards.dev table +
pinout-legend). Continuity checks "to printed 4/5/6" are therefore valid
grounding for GPIO4/5/6 on this board type. Cheap clone variants can differ;
visual double-check when hands-on. Famous silkscreen bugs exist on *third
party adapter boards*, not this one.

## E. Why the two flashes of "working" (Oct 13) are the key

The fault has been intermittent by *measurement*, not by report:
16:39–16:45 idle; 17:18 working (6,719 distinct values, AC RMS 1104); 17:56
mostly idle with periodic bursts; 19:30+ idle. A single stable electronic
failure mode (dead mic) does not self-heal by turning that sharply. A marginal
joint, a marginal mic, or an environmental change (touching/moving wires,
different solder state) does. **Untouched question for Master: what was done
between roughly 16:40 and 17:18 on Oct 13, and was anything to do with the
mic moved again before 17:56?**

## F. Addendum 2026-10-17 (after v8/v9, live board)

Master's objection on record: "degraded mic is not credible; it was working
before." Agreed as stated — no spontaneous degradation. The session-bound
framing stands instead: the last clean capture (2026-10-13 17:18, 6,719
distinct values, RMS 1104) is BEFORE the soldering work; every flat/debris
measurement is AFTER. Candidate mechanisms ranked accordingly:

1. **Module-side joint disturbed during the solder session** (SD/VDD/WS/SCK).
   Best-supported today: the pass-to-pass line-state flicker with no hands
   near the board matches a marginal contact, not a stable dead chip.
2. **VDD lifted during the session while firmware streamed clocks** — the
   datasheet's ESD-diode stress path; session-bound, not spontaneous.
3. **CHIPEN pad exposed/floating** on the module (module-dependent).

Key logic: a powered, enabled INMP441 never leaves the line at all-ones idle —
pure silence is still valid data. Only power/enable/output loss produces
tri-state. DC continuity checks under pressed probes cannot judge a 3 MHz
signal path; only a live-capture reseat loop can.
