## Scope and coordinate convention

Read-only inspection of `build_revision.py`, `render_assembled.py`, `check_assembly.py`, and the saved revision-o assembly report/fastener ledger. **No files changed, scripts executed, or subagents used.** Findings below are nominal CAD deductions—not physical-fit approval.

Use:
- **X:** width.
- **H:** assembled vertical height.
- **D:** forward depth from tub backs; Blender world `Y = −D`.
- Construction coordinates map to assembled `(X,H,D) = (x, module_height+y, module_depth+z)`.

The assembly placements are defined in `render_assembled.py:55–70`.

## Findings, in severity order

### 1. High — Upper exits are parallel but not coaxial; a straight connector-shaped guard would misrepresent the route

References: `build_revision.py:114–119,129–137`; `render_assembled.py:56–64`.

- Main upper exit: **Ø8**, axis along H, at **X=0, D=16**.
- Thermal lower exit: **Ø6**, axis along H, at **X=0, D=8**.
- Their axes therefore have an exact nominal **8 mm depth offset**.

The depth projections of the bores are:
- Main: **D=12…20**.
- Thermal: **D=5…11**.

These intervals are **disjoint by 1 mm**. Even an ideal zero-thickness line parallel to H cannot pass straight through both openings. A rigid straight sleeve aligned to either axis is disqualified.

**Why a dogleg may be needed:** routing must transition 8 mm rearward while crossing a nominal 5 mm exposed inter-module gap. Using wall midplanes, the transition is between approximately `(H=54.5,D=16)` and `(H=62.5,D=8)`—8 mm vertically and 8 mm in depth—but that diagonal traverses portions of both walls. It is not a demonstrated cable path.

A flexible route might bend partly inside the tubs, or bow laterally/outward before returning. Whether any such route works depends on the actual cable, termination, permissible bend radius, and internal component placement. None is established here.

### 2. High — Existing bore attachment cannot simply reuse the current bolt-length assumptions

References: `build_revision.py:188–220`; `fastener_ledger.js:9–20`.

Useful existing rear-facing attachment axes:
- Spine/tub pairs: **X=±18**, at **H=−80, 0, 80**, nominal Ø3.4.
- SHT arm/spine pair: **X=±12, H=61**, nominal Ø3.4.

These are occupied joints, not spare accessory holes.

The ledger assumes:
- Spine/tub: 8 mm grip, 14 mm bolt.
- SHT arm/spine: 10 mm grip, 16 mm bolt.
- Additional washer/nut/thread allowance: 4.4 mm.

Both have only **1.6 mm nominal length margin** under those assumptions. A proposed **2 mm attachment tab** consumes that margin and leaves **−0.4 mm**. Do not specify “reuse existing bolts” without checking the revised stack and actual hardware.

Sharing the SHT pair also means temporarily disturbing the SHT support joint; sharing spine/tub bolts disturbs a structural mounting joint. The guard can remain nonstructural, but its installation must not compromise those joints.

### 3. Medium — A rear bridge at the upper gap runs into the SHT arm unless deliberately offset

References: `build_revision.py:214–220`; `render_assembled.py:60–61`; `revision-o/assembly-checks.json:10–32,70–80`.

Nominal assembled SHT geometry:
- Arm crossbar: **X=−24…80, H=56…66, D=−10…−5**.
- Carrier landing extends to **X=64…96, H=45…77**.
- Carrier itself: **X=64…96, H=45…77, D=3…6**.
- Landing/standoff geometry spans additional depth; the arm’s overall bounds reach **D=3**.

Consequences:
- A central rear guard spanning H=56…61 **cannot occupy the arm crossbar’s D=−10…−5 band**.
- A rear rail must sit **behind D=−10**, with an explicit clearance, or take a different route.
- Routing toward the right-hand carrier risks obstructing its open exposure, strap slots, or fastener access. Keep the cable and guard out of the carrier/landing region—not merely outside the central 16×16 opening.

## Concrete minimal guard options

### Option A — Split, open saddle guards at the facing exits: least intrusive

**Section:** shallow U-channel or rounded saddle, with a separate removable keeper only if needed. Ends terminate outside the walls; no connector-like plug or sleeve projecting into either bore.

**Attachment:** short removable clips engaging existing exterior wall/rim geometry adjacent to the aperture. Clip engagement must stop short of the cover mating plane and corner posts. This is a feasibility option, not a validated snap-fit.

**Function:**
- Protect the exposed span from snagging/contact.
- Provide a broad routing surface.
- Allow cable installation without feeding an unknown connector through a closed guard.

For the upper route, use **two offset saddles**, one at D=16 and one at D=8, rather than a straight joining tube. Leave the transition open for the actual cable route to be established.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Clips intruding into the bore or reducing usable aperture.
- Hooking beneath the lid/face or preventing cover removal.
- Sharp saddle edges or forced bending at the wall.
- A clip requiring deformation or retention strength not yet demonstrated.

This avoids changing existing bolt stacks, but friction/clip retention remains unproven.

### Option B — Rear-mounted open dogleg shield using existing spine bores

**Section:** narrow rear rail with an open, rounded channel reaching forward toward the cable span. Prefer a **removable outboard keeper** over a closed tunnel.

**Attachment:** share a selected existing spine/tub bolt pair; use two points to prevent rotation. Keep the rail central or left of the SHT landing. For an upper rail crossing the SHT crossbar, place its body **behind D=−10**, plus clearance.

**Function:** rear support carries a removable shield over the actual cable route; it does not join the housings or carry module loads.

**Disqualifiers:**
- Rail intersecting the SHT crossbar.
- Forward-reaching arms crossing a lid/face removal path.
- Tabs obstructing rear nuts, mounting/post straps, or wall mounting.
- Added bolt length protruding into electronics space.
- A “dogleg” channel drawn without proving the cable can follow it.

This is more positively retained than clips, but it is **not the smallest part**, and requires hardware-stack revalidation.

### Option C — Detachable saddle on the SHT arm joint

**Attachment:** two rear tabs sharing **X=±12,H=61** bores, with the guard body behind the arm and a central forward-reaching shield.

**Advantage:** nearest existing two-bore attachment to the upper gap.

**Disadvantages/disqualifiers:**
- Disturbs the SHT support joint during installation/removal.
- Cannot preserve the current bolt-length assumption with an arbitrary added tab.
- Must not occupy the tested forward SHT hardware envelopes: radius **3.5 mm**, D=0…3, centered at X=±12,H=61 (`check_assembly.py:100–109`).
- Must not contact the thermal wall or undermine its existing hardware-clearance notches.
- Must remain clear of the SHT landing/carrier and service access.

**Secondary choice only:** convenient location does not make it a free attachment point.

## Recommendation and required checks

Prefer **Option A’s open, removable saddles** if existing wall geometry can retain them without entering the cover interface. Otherwise use **Option B’s rear-mounted shield**, accepting revised fastener verification. Do not bridge the upper exits with a straight decorative sleeve.

Before fixing any channel section dimensions, establish:
1. Actual cable/termination envelope and allowed bends.
2. A continuous upper routing path through both walls and internal spaces.
3. Guard-to-print, guard-to-hardware, and cable-to-edge clearance.
4. Cover removal and driver approach with the guard removed—and, where intended, installed.
5. SHT exposure/access and revised bolt engagement.

**The CAD establishes the 5 mm edge gaps and 8 mm upper-axis offset. It does not establish a usable cable size, safe bend, retention force, or approved attachment.**
