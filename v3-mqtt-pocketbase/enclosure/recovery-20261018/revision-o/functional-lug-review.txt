## Findings, in severity order

### 1. Blocking design constraint: lugs must account for the spine’s rear projection

The main tub’s rear floor is at local **Z = 0–3 mm** (`build_revision.py:79–83,108`). The assembled spine occupies local-equivalent **Z = −5–0 mm**, behind that floor (`build_revision.py:190`; `render_assembled.py:55–69`).

Simply adding ears coplanar with the tub floor would leave their wall-facing surfaces **5 mm forward of the spine’s rear surface**. Tightening those ears directly against a flat wall would require spacers or a different rear offset. This is a geometric mounting conflict, not evidence of a particular failure load.

### 2. Important uncertainty: wall contact and hardware access remain unvalidated

The SHT arm is placed farther rearward than the spine, at assembly depth −10 mm (`render_assembled.py:60–61`), and includes a landing extending sideways (`build_revision.py:214–220`). Consequently, making lug backs flush with the spine does **not** establish that the complete assembly can sit against a flat wall.

Wall spacers or a mounting substrate with suitable clearance may still be necessary. The wall anchor, head/washer envelope, driver access, installation sequence and rear nut clearance also need definition. These cannot be inferred from the poster.

### 3. Missing verification: existing digital gates do not establish mounting functionality

The builder checks manifold edges and positive volume (`build_revision.py:298–310`). The assembly audit explicitly excludes physical fit and general screw-head/nut/tool access (`check_assembly.py:42–44`). Neither establishes lug-root continuity, mounting-tool clearance, a wall-clearance envelope or mechanical capacity.
