## Findings — severity order

Paths below are relative to `v3-mqtt-pocketbase/enclosure/recovery-20261018/`.

### 1. Medium — Summary accepts contradictory or empty relief sample evidence
**`summarize_verification.js:14`**

The new gate checks the two revision labels, their aggregate `pass` values, and both current STL hashes, but **never validates either result’s `samples`**.

Consequently, a report containing N `pass:false`, O `pass:true`, and correct hashes passes even if:
- O has zero samples;
- O contains failing samples;
- N contains only passing samples;
- the sampled coordinates or clearance threshold differ from the intended probe.

Hash freshness establishes which file bytes the report claims to describe; it does not establish that its aggregate conclusions follow from its evidence. This matters because packaging invokes the summarizer without rerunning the probe (`package_revision_o.sh:6`).

**Recommendation:** Require the exact expected sample set, finite measurements, and per-sample outcomes consistent with the threshold; recompute both aggregates rather than trusting the stored booleans.

### 2. Medium — A broken negative-control probe counts as the expected N failure
**`check_strap_relief.py:18–22,29–30`**

A ray miss becomes `pass:false`, and N is considered a successful negative control whenever **any** sample fails. Therefore, missing geometry, an incorrectly positioned N mesh, or an incomplete import can satisfy the negative-control condition without demonstrating the intended obstructed escape channel.

O still must pass all 18 rays, so this is not an unconditional O bypass. It does, however, undermine the stated control: the checker does not distinguish **“N was measured and obstructed”** from **“N could not be measured.”**

**Recommendation:** Separate probe validity from clearance success. Require all expected N rays to hit valid geometry, then require measured clearance failures at explicitly intended control locations.

### 3. Low — The clearance predicate has no lower geometry bound
**`check_strap_relief.py:19–21`**

Any hit at `z <= 1.85` passes, including a floor far below the nominal carrier. For example, displaced geometry below the intended part can produce arbitrarily large reported clearance and still satisfy this probe.

This is narrower than continuous-route or physical-fit limitations: even the discrete measurement lacks a sanity check establishing that the hit is an expected carrier floor. Other export checks may catch some displacement or dimensional errors, but this checker itself does not establish that relationship.

**Recommendation:** Validate the expected floor-height interval or expected carrier bounds alongside the upper clearance threshold.
