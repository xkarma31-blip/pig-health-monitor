## Recommendation

Use a **rear board-plane carrier**, with the sensor package projecting forward through an opening—not a smaller bridge placed in front of the pupil. Keep the enclosure mounting interface, but replace the unsupported central span with **board-specific rear seating surfaces and positive retention**.

This is a conditional architecture recommendation, **not optical or hardware-fit approval**. The published dimensions you supplied establish useful envelope cases, but not mounting-hole positions, underside keepouts, package location, or entrance-pupil position.

Read-only review completed against the actual `build_revision.py`, `render_assembled.py`, and existing planar optical-budget checker. No files modified; no subagents used.

## Findings, in severity order

### 1. High — Existing cradle does not seat the cited small boards

**References:** `build_revision.py:259–262`, also `build_revision.py:180–186`.

The raised cradle has a **46 × 26 mm opening**. In their stated orientations:

- Adafruit PCB **25.4 × 17.78 mm** fits entirely within that opening.
- SparkFun **25.4 × 25.4 mm** likewise fits.
- Waveshare **28 × 16 mm** likewise fits.

The surrounding ring therefore provides no intended rear PCB bearing surface for these centered envelopes. Strap slots at x = ±27 mm do not solve this by themselves: retention needs an explicit contact/load path, not a strap spanning an unsupported board.

**Next-design requirement:** show actual rear contact patches, a forward-retention feature, and an anti-slide feature. Do not infer usable bearing areas from rectangular PCB outlines alone.

### 2. High — A forward 42 × 12 mm bridge trades mechanical support for wide-FOV obstruction

**Reference:** `check_thermal_bridge_optical_budget.js:3–11`.

For a hypothetical centered pupil, perpendicular aperture, and 8 mm pupil-to-aperture distance:

- Wide **110° × 75°** nominal field requires half-extents of approximately **11.43 × 6.14 mm**.
- The opening supplies **21 × 6 mm** half-clearance.

Thus the vertical nominal edge ray already fails by approximately **0.14 mm**, even with zero manufacturing/alignment margin. With the checker’s 1 mm edge allowance, the deficit is approximately **1.14 mm**.

This result is conditional on that simplified geometry. It does **not** prove the assembled tilted hood clears or clips identically.

**Next-design requirement:** put seating material behind the candidate pupil, and evaluate every remaining forward obstruction independently.

### 3. Medium — Current geometry uses a tilted platform and a differently defined aperture

**References:** `build_revision.py:263–269`, `build_revision.py:281–283`; `render_assembled.py:55–69`.

The raised platform is rotated **15° about X**, translated, and baked into its exported coordinates. The hood roof slopes at 15°, but its **46 × 26 mm opening is cut by an unrotated box**.

Consequently, “roof opening setback” is not one universal axial distance, and 46 × 26 is not automatically an aperture measured perpendicular to the sensor axis. A centered perpendicular-plane calculation is only a screening test.

The assembled mapping is:

\[
(x,y,z)_\text{construction}\rightarrow(x,-17-z,80+y)_\text{world}
\]

for the hood and raised cradle. A new carrier must use this mapping consistently; applying another 15° rotation in the renderer would double-tilt already baked geometry.

### 4. Medium — Appearance review does not establish board fit or optical clearance

**References:** `render_assembled.py:55–85`, `build_revision.py:298–319`.

The assembly imports the printed components but provides no thermal PCB/package/pupil proxy here. The mesh gate checks manifoldness and positive volume—not PCB retention, component collisions, insertion access, or optical occlusion.

Also, the older `thermal_strap_carrier` remains positioned in the assembly but is hidden for rendering at `render_assembled.py:71`. Future collision tests must explicitly classify it as inactive rather than accidentally testing both carrier alternatives.

**Missing tests:** transformed PCB/package envelopes, supported contact areas, insertion/removal, fastening access, and full-assembly optical intersection tests.

## Minimal coherent parametric experiment

### A. One shared mount, interchangeable rear-support inserts

Retain the existing mounting pattern **x = ±30, y = ±9 mm** (`build_revision.py:270–276`) as the experiment’s enclosure interface.

Test one carrier architecture:

1. Outer mounting frame and standoffs.
2. Two rear bearing rails or several rear seating pads.
3. Board-specific locating stops.
4. Removable edge retainers, positioned only over verified component-free PCB edges.
5. Central package/underside keepout.

Use interchangeable inserts for the three PCB envelopes rather than a supposedly universal clamp. Mounting holes and contact patches remain provisional until the corresponding board drawings or measurements establish them.

The support should carry the PCB from its **rear face**, while the package projects toward the hood. Avoid a retaining crossbar across the sensor’s forward field.

### B. Explicit local planes

Define a sensor-local frame:

- \(u\): PCB horizontal axis.
- \(v\): PCB vertical axis.
- \(w\): forward optical-axis direction.
- PCB front face: \(w=0\).
- PCB rear face: \(w=-t_\mathrm{PCB}\).
- Rear support material: at or behind \(w=-t_\mathrm{PCB}\), except explicitly modeled edge-retention features.
- Hypothetical pupil: \(P=(\delta_u,\delta_v,p)\).

For correspondence with the existing tilted platform, use:

\[
r(u,v,w)=O+R_x(15^\circ)(u,v,w)
\]

where \(O\) is the selected **PCB-front-plane origin**, not an ambiguously named platform origin. Then apply the renderer’s assembly transform.

Do not derive \(p\) from the supplied **16 mm narrow / 10.5 mm wide overall product heights**. Those are envelope constraints, not pupil measurements.

### C. Small, explicit sweep

Use the following as **hypothetical sensitivity cases**, not claimed device dimensions:

- PCB envelopes: **25.4 × 17.78**, **25.4 × 25.4**, **28 × 16 mm**.
- Pupil positions relative to PCB front:  
  \(p=\{-2,0,2,4,8,12\}\) mm.
- Lateral pupil offsets:  
  \(\delta_u,\delta_v=\{-2,0,+2\}\) mm.
- Nominal FOV cases: **55° × 35°**, **110° × 75°**.
- Hypothetical roof-center axial setbacks: **0, 2, 4, 6, 8 mm**.
- Report clearance both without allowance and with **1 mm geometric allowance**.

Board placement, pupil position, and roof setback are linked: choose a pupil case and setback, then solve the board translation. **Do not vary all three independently.** Reject mechanically impossible placements rather than retaining their optical results.

The negative pupil case deliberately challenges the assumption that every rear-support feature is behind the optical origin.

### D. Geometric gates

**Mechanical first**

- Each board has positive-area intended bearing contacts; no reliance on edge coincidence.
- Rear components, solder joints, connectors and cable bends clear the support.
- Retainers prevent forward lift and lateral escape without crossing sensitive components.
- Insertion and tool-access paths exist.
- Carrier, fasteners and package clear the tub, separator and hood.
- Report disconnected mesh islands and minimum load-bearing ligaments, not just manifoldness.

**Optical second**

For a perpendicular screening plane a distance \(d\ge0\) ahead of the pupil:

\[
a_u \ge |\delta_u|+d\tan(H/2)+m
\]
\[
a_v \ge |\delta_v|+d\tan(V/2)+m
\]

Use this only where the plane and axis assumptions hold.

For the actual assembly:

- Transform pupil and ray directions into the same frame as the meshes.
- Test forward rays against **all active printed parts and fastener envelopes**.
- Include aperture entrance and exit surfaces: thickness matters.
- Check edge/corner rays and a field grid; corners alone can miss internal obstructions.
- For a stronger geometric gate, test mesh intersection against the filled rectangular angular frustum, with the chosen clearance allowance.
- Report the obstructing part, location and angular case—not merely “FOV passed.”

An entirely rearward support cannot intersect this idealized forward frustum. That is the architectural advantage—but edge clamps, a recessed pupil, hood walls and package geometry still require testing.

## Decision rule

Advance the rear-support concept only if at least one **mechanically feasible** board placement clears the selected hypothetical optical cases with stated margins. Record failures and the feasible range.

The defensible outcome is:

> “This insert supports this PCB envelope and clears these modeled pupil/FOV/offset cases.”

It is **not**:

> “Rear mounting makes all MLX90640 variants optically compatible.”

The smallest useful next experiment is therefore **one rear-carrier concept, three board inserts, and one shared mechanical/optical parameter sweep**—not another forward bridge or a full enclosure redesign.
