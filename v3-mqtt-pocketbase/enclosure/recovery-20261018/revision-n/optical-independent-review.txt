## Specific checks

- **Common frame is consistent.**  
  `check_optical_cone.py:11–16` places the face at `z=-3…0`, tub at `z=-17…-3`, and hood/cradle at their construction coordinates. This matches the thermal assembly offsets: tub `0`, face `14`, hood/cradle `17`, after subtracting the shared `17 mm` assembly translation. The shifts are not an accidental extra displacement.

- **Roof-normal axis has the correct sign.**  
  The roof is constructed as `z=16+y*tan(15°)` (`build_revision.py:231–234`). Its outward normal is proportional to `(0,-tan(15°),1)`, matching `check_optical_cone.py:19`. There is no missing additional 15° rotation.

- **The basis is orthonormal, although its vertical sign is unconventional.**  
  `horizontal.cross(axis)` produces `(0,-cos(15°),-sin(15°))` (`check_optical_cone.py:20–21`). This makes `(horizontal, vertical, axis)` left-handed. **That does not invalidate these tests:** the rectangular sampling and offset sets are symmetric, and both angular signs are tested. It does mean signed “vertical” and “yaw” labels should not automatically be interpreted using a conventional camera coordinate system.

- **Alignment rotates the whole ray basis correctly.**  
  `check_optical_alignment.py:19–25` rotates the complete direction, not merely its central axis. Pupil position stays fixed during the pointing sweep, a coherent choice for angular sensitivity. Because no case combines nonzero yaw and pitch, rotation-order concerns do not affect the published cases.

- **No demonstrated `Object.ray_cast` coordinate-space misuse.**  
  The call at `check_optical_cone.py:33` is Blender’s object-space ray API. The script imports STL meshes into an empty scene and applies assembly offsets directly to vertices rather than object locations. With the expected identity import transforms, ray coordinates and mesh coordinates therefore share the same space. This is **not** the erroneous pattern of passing world rays to arbitrarily transformed objects. Nor does the call require a CAD-solid intersection API to answer triangle obstruction.

- **Counting is explicitly distinguished.**  
  `check_optical_cone.py:32–38` counts one hit per part per sampled ray; `blocked_count` can therefore exceed the number of blocked rays. `unique_blocked_rays` correctly deduplicates samples, and the report states the distinction. Alignment’s `any(...)` (`check_optical_alignment.py:26,49`) correctly counts each blocked ray once.

- **Scope statements are honest.**  
  `revision-n/MANUFACTURER_RESEARCH.md:17–21,76–93` explicitly rejects hard-boundary FOV interpretation, universal safe setbacks, measured mounting tolerance, and exhaustive combined extremes. Its 30-case and 60-case totals match the loops. Fractions such as `77/441` describe **sampled rays**, not sensitivity-weighted throughput or percentage loss of physical FOV.

**Bottom line:** no supported basis for declaring the results invalid because of transforms or the ray API. The concrete weaknesses are missing regression controls and provenance—not an identified geometric computation bug.
